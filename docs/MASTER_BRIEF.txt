2. DOCUMENTARY STORYTELLING
==================================================

THIS IS OUR MOST IMPORTANT REQUIREMENT.

The narration must sound like an experienced teacher, presenter and documentary storyteller explaining an interesting story to an audience.

Imagine someone sitting in front of an audience and explaining:

How did this company begin?
What happened next?
Why did the founder make that decision?
What went wrong?
How did the company overcome the problem?
What can we learn from this story?

The narration must be conversational, intelligent, natural and engaging.

STRICTLY AVOID:

- Robotic AI narration.
- Reading the script like a textbook.
- Reading facts one after another without explanation.
- Extremely fast narration.
- Continuous sentences without natural pauses.
- Unnecessary complicated vocabulary.
- Repetitive explanations.
- Artificial motivational speeches.
- Overdramatic storytelling.
- Excessive dramatic sound effects.

REQUIRED NARRATION STYLE:

Explain the story naturally.

For example:

"Now, here's something interesting.

McDonald's wasn't originally the massive global company we know today.

It started with a much simpler idea.

Two brothers wanted to make restaurant service faster, more affordable and more efficient.

But here's where the story gets interesting...

A businessman named Ray Kroc saw something in their small restaurant that would eventually change the entire fast-food industry."

This is the type of conversational storytelling we want.

The narrator should:
- Explain complicated events in simple language.
- Give the audience enough time to understand.
- Use natural pauses.
- Emphasize important words.
- Change vocal expression according to the story.
- Sound interested in the subject.
- Build curiosity before revealing important events.
- Explain WHY something happened, not just WHAT happened.

The audience should never feel rushed.

- Avoid repetitive dramatic music.
- Avoid excessive sound effects.
- Avoid loud transitions.

VOICE CLARITY MUST ALWAYS HAVE PRIORITY.

5. VOX-INSPIRED ANIMATED TIMELINES
==================================================

THIS IS A MAJOR REQUIREMENT.

We want professional animated timelines similar to high-quality explanatory documentaries.

When a story moves between different years, locations or historical events, create a properly visible animated timeline.

TIMELINE DESIGN:

- Use a clean horizontal timeline.
- Show a clear connecting line.
- Use visible location pins or event markers.
- Highlight the current year or event.
- Animate the movement from one event to another.
- Display relevant dates and short descriptions.
- Use our permanent brand colors.
- Maintain consistent line thickness and typography.

IMPORTANT:

Do not create a timeline where only the current pin is visible and the previous information disappears immediately.

The timeline should be properly composed and readable.

When moving from one location or event to another:

1. Show the starting point.
2. Show the connecting line.
3. Animate the journey.
4. Reveal the next location or event.
5. Give the viewer enough time to understand the transition.

Do not create awkward slider effects where previous timeline elements are unnecessarily cut off.

The complete timeline should look intentionally designed and professionally animated.

Use timelines whenever they improve historical understanding.

==================================================

- Company expansion.
- International locations.
- Founder's journey.
- New market entries.
- International business operations.
- Global competition.
- Factory and headquarters locations.

Map animation requirements:

- Accurate geography.
- Smooth camera movement.
- Clearly visible location markers.
- Animated routes and connecting lines.
- Country and city labels.
- Consistent brand colors.

For example, in a McDonald's documentary:

Show where the original restaurant was established.

Use animated connecting routes to explain expansion.

Do not simply display a static world map.

The audience should visually understand how the company expanded.
