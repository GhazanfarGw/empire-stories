# MONEY ATLAS · Episode 01 · "How Dubai Got Rich Without Oil" — fact sheet (verified 21 Nov 2026)
Central question: Dubai has almost no oil left in its economy. So where does its money come from, and could other countries copy it?

## Before oil: pearls (to ~1930s)
- Pearling was a summer industry; boats left ports such as Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Doha and Manama. It ran on borrowed money: merchants advanced cash to boat captains. (Qatar Digital Library, "Pearl Diving in the Gulf 1840s–1930s")
- Lorimer counted 335 pearl boats at Dubai, 410 at Abu Dhabi, 350 at Doha (early 1900s). (QDL)
- Collapse: cultured pearls from Japan (pioneered by Mikimoto Kōkichi) plus the Great Depression destroyed the trade in the late 1920s–1930s. (QDL "British Government Resist Modernisation…"; The National; RUDN journal)

## Turning points (dated)
- 1959: Dubai Creek dredged; Dubai airport opens. (Timeline of Dubai)
- 1966: oil found offshore at the Fateh field. (Timeline of Dubai; Emirates.com history)
- 1979: Jebel Ali port and the Dubai World Trade Centre open. (Emirates.com history)
- 9 Jan 1985: decree establishing the Jebel Ali Free Zone. March 1985: Emirates airline established; 25 Oct 1985: first flight, to Karachi. (Timeline of Dubai)

## Oil fades
- 1980s: oil ≈ half of Dubai's GDP (Gulf News). 2004: Dubai official says oil fell from 54% in the early 1980s to below 7%.
- 2016: hydrocarbons 1.3% of Dubai GDP (Oxford Business Group). 2017: "less than one per cent" (Sheikh Ahmed bin Saeed, Dubai Economic Development Committee, via Zawya).
- Today: "over 95% of Dubai's GDP is non-oil"; real GDP $120.6B in 2024, growth 3.2% (dubai.ae, official portal).

## The 2009 crash
- 25 Nov 2009: Dubai World asks creditors for a standstill on ~$26B of debt, mainly linked to property units Nakheel and Limitless; markets shaken; biggest government default risk since Argentina 2001 (Wharton; Reuters via Financial Express).
- 14 Dec 2009: Abu Dhabi provides $10B; $4.1B used to repay Nakheel's Islamic bond; Dubai's market opens up 10.1% (Al Jazeera; WBUR/AP; Reuters).

## Now (2025–2026)
- 2025 (first nine months): GDP AED 355B, +4.7% (WAM, official).
- H1 2026: real GDP ≈ AED 473B, down 2.3% from ≈ AED 484B in H1 2025 (Dubai Data and Statistics Establishment via Khaleej Times; series recalibrated). Financial sector +8.8%; largest sectors: wholesale & retail trade 20.5%, finance & insurance 14%, real estate 11.8%, transport & storage 10.7% (Entrepreneur ME). Note: give the decline as reported; do not speculate on causes beyond the source.
